# Rebuild Mini Waterways around locks, not boats

**Build a Motorways-shaped game with a water-native twist: static canal infrastructure that the player lays down, autonomous barges that use it, and locks (a scarce, time-limited throughput bottleneck) as the equivalent of the road junction.** Mini Metro and Mini Motorways share a small, well-documented core: one verb, autonomous agents, procedural maps, a weekly two-option reward, a scarce "break the geography" resource, and a visible local overload that ends the run once a timer fills. The theme (trains, cars), the shape vocabulary, the city maps and even the exact fail rule are incidental. Almost every numeric tuning value is missing from reliable sources, so Gijs must treat them as playtest parameters rather than facts: the two Mini Metro overcrowding timers conflict (10 s vs 45 s), the Motorways pin thresholds (7 square, 10 round) are unverified community figures, and no spawn-rate curve exists anywhere. The existing Mini-Waterways repo is about 4.3k lines of Vite and vanilla Canvas with no tests, no assets and several unused dependencies, so the rebuild should start in a fresh project and keep only the Pages workflow and a few conventions. The recommended stack is TypeScript, Vite, a pure headless simulation module with a seeded RNG, Canvas 2D first (PixiJS only if profiling demands it), and a small hand-rolled Web Audio layer. Build in six phases, with a playable tracer bullet in phase 1 and the water-specific mechanics layered on afterwards. Use a distinct title, palette and shape language, because Dinosaur Polo Club (DPC) has taken legal action against close clones.

## What the two games actually are, and how sure we are

Mini Metro is a line-drawing game: stations of different shapes appear at random, passengers (small shapes) want to reach a station of their own shape rather than a specific location, trains run automatically, and the game ends when a station stays overcrowded for too long, signalled by a countdown circle ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Metro_(video_game))). Time can be paused to reroute, and each in-game week grants new assets from a choice of two, such as a carriage, a new line, a tunnel or bridge pair, or an interchange ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Metro_(video_game)); [hamy.xyz strategy guide](https://hamy.xyz/blog/2024-03_mini-metro-best-upgrades)). Rivers must be crossed with a limited supply of tunnels and bridges ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Metro_(video_game))). Mini Motorways swaps the line for a tile-grid road: coloured houses (each with cars) must reach same-coloured destinations, which accumulate "pins" (trip requests); too many pins start a warning timer and a full timer ends the run ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Motorways)). Its weekly reward always includes road tiles and usually one special tool: bridges, tunnels, traffic lights, roundabouts or motorways ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Motorways)). Score is trips completed.

The confidence split matters for the rebuild. Confirmed by developer or official sources: the weekly two-choice reward, overflow-timer failure, the Mini Metro mode set (Normal, Extreme, Endless, Creative) and the Motorways mode set (Classic, Endless, Expert, Creative). Expert was explicitly inspired by Mini Metro Extreme, and Endless removes the time limit and earns upgrades through milestones ([press release](https://gamespress.com/Mini-Motorways-Builds-Highway-to-Two-New-Game-Modes-with-The-Endless-E)). Reviewer or community estimates: a Motorways week of about 2.5 minutes, a pin warning timer of roughly a minute, and about 20 road tiles per bundle ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/review/mini-motorways/contemplative-commute); [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-mini-motorways/)). The Mini Metro capacity of about six passengers per station and the train capacity of six (twelve with one carriage) come from Wikipedia and a fan guide respectively ([hamy.xyz](https://hamy.xyz/blog/2024-03_mini-metro-best-upgrades)). My inferences, not sourced: a station's overcrowding timer fills while the count is at or above capacity and drains below it; Motorways failure is per destination and resets when pins fall; spawning is a ramped global rate with a free-footprint search. Motorways spawning has some community-documented rules (nothing spawns within one tile of a building or on a road, same-colour houses cluster, destinations sit far from them, and if no valid spot exists the game raises demand at an existing destination instead), all from player and reviewer observation ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1127500/discussions/0/4410795316954451666); [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-mini-motorways/)).

The known gaps are real and should not be papered over. The Mini Metro overcrowding timer is reported as 10 seconds by one source and 45 seconds by another, with neither page opened by the researchers ([Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Mini_Metro); [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/mini-metro/review/)). Not found anywhere: week length in Mini Metro, spawn curves, per-shape probabilities, interchange capacity, train and car speeds, and the Motorways Tier-2 upgrade trigger. The Fandom wikis returned HTTP 402 and GDC talks were gated, so those are the first places to check manually. The one developer statement about hidden complexity is useful rather than a gap: the passenger and train AI is "far and away the most complex thing in Mini Metro" and the part players do not control ([Digitally Downloaded](https://www.digitallydownloaded.net/?p=44565)). For Gijs's design that argues for agents that are simple enough to explain in one sentence.

## The essence is a short list, and the rest is paint

The design principles that recur, and which I would call essential, are these. The constraints were set to limit scope (minimal art, no hand-built levels), which forced procedural generation ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Metro_(video_game))). The player designs infrastructure and watches autonomous units use it rather than commanding them ([Super Jump Magazine](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/from-game-jam-idea-to-mobile-classic/)). The developers describe the result as "predictable chaos", with the crisis point deliberately built in because the game has to end somewhere ([Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/dinosaur-polo-club/interview)). Reviewers confirm the arc: "At its heart, Mini Motorways is about failure", with each attempt ratcheting up until inevitable collapse ([OpenCritic aggregation](https://opencritic.com/game/11703/-/reviews)). Audio is a feedback channel, not decoration: the Motorways soundtrack uses the gameplay as the music's conductor, and car horns are deliberately "just annoying enough" to signal growing trouble ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/turning-road-noise-into-music-mini-motorways)). Mini Metro's audio is nearly all samples triggered by events and quantised to a per-city harmonic framework ([Designing Sound](https://designingsound.org/2016/02/18/the-programmed-music-of-mini-metro-interview-with-rich-vreeland-disasterpeace)).

The incidental list is equally clear from the evidence. Motorways deliberately traded some of Metro's abstraction for recognisable buildings and landscape at some cost to clarity ([Super Jump Magazine](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/from-game-jam-idea-to-mobile-classic/)), and in 2023 it received Metro's trains and stations as an interchangeable module ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Motorways)). The map set (34 Metro cities, 27 Motorways cities), the shape alphabet and the "pins" scoring unit are all swappable. The two games differ in who owns what: Metro makes the player own the lines and schedule, Motorways makes the player own static infrastructure while agents route themselves, so failure feels like emergent congestion rather than a wrong plan (my inference, consistent with the sources). One reviewer found Metro's auto-pathing turned problems into "an annoyance to fix rather than a fun puzzle", while Motorways' freely drawn roads gave more control ([RAWG review snippet](https://rawg.io/games/mini-motorways/reviews)). The same sources flag the traps to avoid: traffic lights were widely criticised as making traffic worse ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Motorways)), and delayed feedback with no rerouting feels unfair, with spawn blocking the dominant exploit ([Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-mini-motorways/)). A water game should make every weekly option genuinely useful and should not let players trivially wall off the spawner. Note that "no tutorial text", "slow-fail" and "one more run" are consistent with the sources but were not found as developer statements, so they are my reading and not facts.

## A lock-driven canal game, with water supply as phase-two depth

Five concept directions were evaluated: Lock Flight (throughput puzzle), Summit Water Budget (water as a visible resource), Tidal Harbour (time-varying flow), Flood and Irrigation (flow network without agents), and Ferries/Bridges/Open Spans (multimodal crossings). The research notes suggest combining the first two, and I agree, with one adjustment: use a Motorways-style grid and static canal tiles, not a free-form line as in Metro. The reasons are that tile-grid pathfinding is far cheaper to build than Metro's octilinear line routing, that the existing repo already experimented with a grid, and that a solo developer benefits from the genre's lower-risk half. Real lock facts supply the central mechanic: a chamber must fill or drain, open its gates, take a boat, close and level, with a full operation reported at 10 to 20 minutes in the real world, and meeting an oncoming boat saves 5 to 10 minutes ([Wikipedia: Lock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock_(water_transport))). In-game that becomes a lock with a fixed cycle time, one boat per direction at a time, and a bonus when opposing traffic shares a cycle. Canals above sea level also genuinely need feeder water from rivers, reservoirs or back-pumping ([Wikipedia: Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal)), which justifies the second layer.

The proposed game, which I will call by the working title **Waterways** (confirm this is intended), reads as follows. Coloured wharves (sources, the house analogue) spawn barges; matching coloured quays (destinations, the pin analogue) request cargo. The player lays canal tiles on a procedurally generated map of lowland, hills and rivers. Crossing an elevation step requires a lock, which acts as the junction, and is the scarce item: the map generator guarantees that every run needs locks, exactly as rivers force bridges in the parent games. Barges pick a shortest path at departure and do not reroute (matching the Motorways behaviour reported by one source, so consider whether to copy the "no repath" rule, since the same source calls it unfair), then queue at locks. Failure is local: when a quay's waiting-cargo count passes its capacity, a visible ring timer starts, and a full ring ends the run. Each in-game week the player picks one of two bundles of canal tiles plus a tool, drawn from a pool that includes a second lock chamber, a side pond (raises lock throughput or saves water, which is real lock practice ([Wikipedia: Lock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock_(water_transport)))), an aqueduct or boat lift that skips a lock, and a dredger. Phase two adds the Summit Water Budget: each lock cycle drains a summit pound, reservoirs and feeders refill it on a seasonal rain curve, and one single gauge shows it. The research notes warn that two coupled systems may overload newcomers, so ship it as an optional second layer and keep the base game playable without it.

Alternatives, with the honest trade-offs. Ferries/Bridges (direction 5) is the cheapest to build but edges toward a Motorways reskin. Tidal Harbour (3) has the best audio hook, since tides give a pulse, but "wait for a window" can feel passive. Flood and Irrigation (4) needs no agent routing, which is cheaper, but it loses the charm of watching small vehicles move. No commercial canal-lock puzzle in this genre was found, but the search was weak because Steam pages were unreadable, so "unoccupied niche" is unverified. The earlier Mini-Waterways concept (ports, passengers, clean-water stations, sustainability scoring) adds feature lists but, per the notes, no tension core; a boat-and-port prototype on itch.io ends after a fixed 50 cycles with no escalation, which is the typical failure mode to avoid ([itch.io](https://brpasiliao.itch.io/mini-ferry)).

Tuning values to start from, all labelled as playtest knobs rather than facts: a overflow capacity near 7 to 10 units per quay (community figure for Motorways, unverified); a warning timer of 30 to 60 seconds (reviewer estimate for Motorways, with Metro's 10 s versus 45 s conflict bracketing it); a week of about 2.5 minutes (reviewer estimate); and roughly 20 tiles per weekly bundle (reviewer estimate). Spawn rate should ramp geometrically, since a reviewer notes traffic grows geometrically so every run ends ([Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-mini-motorways/)). Expose all of these in one config module and drive the endgame by a single global difficulty multiplier. The modes can follow the parents: Classic, Endless (no fail, upgrades via milestones), Expert (permanent placement), and a shared-seed daily challenge, the last being cheap because the sim is seeded.

## Stack and architecture: a pure simulation with swappable skins

Recommended stack: TypeScript and Vite, a headless simulation module, Canvas 2D rendering, hand-rolled Web Audio, Vitest for tests, and GitHub Pages via Actions. Tool versions read on 2026-10-22: Vite 8.3.5, TypeScript 7.0.2, Vitest 5.0.5 and PixiJS 8.22.0 ([Vite](https://api.github.com/repos/vitejs/vite/releases/latest); [TypeScript](https://registry.npmjs.org/typescript/latest); [Vitest](https://registry.npmjs.org/vitest/latest); [PixiJS](https://api.github.com/repos/pixijs/pixijs/releases/latest)). Pin to what `npm install` resolves on the day and check release notes, since plugin compatibility with Vite 8 (for example vite-plugin-pwa) is unconfirmed. Canvas 2D is sufficient for the entity counts involved (tens of locks and quays, tens of barges, a tile grid), provided the static map layer is cached to its own canvas and the dynamic layer alone is redrawn, following the MDN HiDPI and layering advice ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Optimizing_canvas)). That sufficiency claim is my inference: no Canvas-versus-Pixi benchmark was found, so do a one-day spike if the first prototype stutters. PixiJS v8 has an experimental Canvas 2D fallback renderer from 8.16.0 ([PixiJS blog](https://pixijs.com/blog/8.16.0)), so a later move should not be painful if the renderer stays behind a thin interface. Phaser is overkill for a vector game with no sprites or physics, and SVG DOM is the weakest choice for many moving entities. UI chrome (menus, week-choice cards) can be plain DOM around the canvas.

The architecture is the main decision. Keep `src/sim` free of DOM, `Date.now()` and `Math.random()`: it takes `(state, inputs, dt)` and a seeded RNG and returns the new state, while `render`, `audio` and `ui` read it. The reference repos show the pattern working at small scale: tinytraffictown has a headless `Simulation`, a `Renderer`, `Input` and `Storage`, plus autosave ([tinytraffictown](https://github.com/pyrou/tinytraffictown)), and svgmetro mirrors a headless simulation to SVG ([svgmetro](https://github.com/ujjwalvivek/svgmetro)). Use a fixed-timestep accumulator loop with a clamped frame time and render interpolation ([Gaffer On Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)); pause and fast-forward scale the accumulator input, never `dt`. Use sfc32 seeded by an xmur3 string hash for the PRNG, which supports daily seeds from a date string ([bryc PRNGs](https://github.com/bryc/code/blob/master/jshash/PRNGs.md)), with separate streams for spawns and cosmetics. Barge pathing is A* over the canal graph (Red Blob Games: A* for a single target, BFS or Dijkstra when costs are uniform or varied ([Red Blob Games](https://www.redblobgames.com/pathfinding/a-star/introduction.html))) with lock nodes carrying a queue and cycle timer; recompute only on edit via a dirty flag. State is plain serialisable objects using ids, so a save is a snapshot with a version field, and a replay is the seed plus an input log with a periodic state hash. Floating-point determinism across browsers is unverified, so avoid transcendental functions in the sim if leaderboard-grade dailies matter, and note that this is an engineering inference.

Input uses Pointer Events with `setPointerCapture` and `touch-action: none`, with generous hit radii (24 to 32 CSS px) for touch; these come from general knowledge and were not fetched. Audio follows the scheduling pattern documented on web.dev: a roughly 25 ms timer scheduling notes in a roughly 100 ms lookahead window on the Web Audio clock ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/audio-scheduling)), with the context created or resumed inside a user gesture ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices)). A cheap version of the DPC idea: a pentatonic scale chosen from the day's seed, a quantised soft note per delivery or lock cycle, a pad whose filter tracks a 0 to 1 tension value from quay crowding, and a distinct soft cue for the ring timer. That design is my proposal, not a documented recipe. Implement mute and volume controls from the start, plus a colourblind-safe palette, since both parents ship colourblind modes ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/287980/Mini_Metro/); [press release](https://gamespress.com/Mini-Motorways-Builds-Highway-to-Two-New-Game-Modes-with-The-Endless-E)).

## What to keep or drop from the existing repo, and where the IP lines are

The repository is a Vite and vanilla Canvas 2D project of **4,295 lines of TypeScript** in 35 tracked files, MIT-licensed, 22 commits, last active 2025-12-10 ([repo](https://github.com/CaptainPancakeWithBacon/Mini-Waterways)). It has no tests, no art or audio, about 53 scattered hex colours, `strict: false`, a pinned TypeScript 4.8, and unused dependencies (three, neataptic, color-namer, plus webpack leftovers); a 980-line `Start.ts` scene holds state, input and rendering together; and the README says "Current Features: None" while contradicting itself on dependencies (repo audit of the same URL). The recommendation is to rebuild in a fresh directory and archive the old code as reference. Keep the GitHub Pages workflow (`npm ci`, build, `upload-pages-artifact`, `deploy-pages`) after bumping Node to a current LTS and restricting triggers to `main`, since the existing one also deploys from `development`. Keep the `.editorconfig`, and keep the idea of a rAF game loop, but reimplement the loop, input and grid-snap helpers (they are thin or coupled; `Grid.ts` imports a type from `Boat.ts`). Drop webpack, ts-loader, the dev-server, three, neataptic, color-namer, the Netlify config unless wanted, the committed `.idea` and `.vscode` folders, and all ESLint 8 and airbnb configuration in favour of a current flat config. Add `typecheck`, `lint` and `test` scripts and run them in CI.

One provenance issue needs resolving before any verbatim reuse: the loop, input and canvas helpers appear to come from a school-supplied engine (an `@author` tag, the HZ University author field, and a README credit to erjbroek), so the real licence is unverified even though the repo shows MIT. Since reimplementing the loop is about 100 lines, the lazy and safe path is to write fresh code and not copy it. The same applies to outside repos: permissive ones (svgmetro MIT; mini-metro-js Apache-2.0; TinySubway MIT) can be adapted with attribution, while repos with no LICENSE file, such as tinytraffictown and RyanEdo/mini-metro-web, are all-rights-reserved by default and should be read for ideas only ([GitHub search results in the technical notes](https://github.com/pyrou/tinytraffictown)). None of the surveyed clones is a quality benchmark; most have fewer than 10 stars.

On IP, this is not legal advice. Game mechanics and genre ideas are generally not protected the way names, logos, art, music and code are. The practical evidence is that DPC does act on close copies: Mini Subway was removed from the Nintendo Switch eShop, with DPC saying it will "take legal action" if needed after trying the developer first ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2022/08/mini-metro-copycat-game-has-been-removed-from-switch-eshop)). By contrast Mini Airways (2024) keeps a different domain and says it shares no code or art, though commenters still called its name and style plagiarism ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/03/mini-airways-is-like-mini-metro-mini-motorways-but-for-aircraft)). The safe-borrow list is autonomous agents, weekly choice, overflow timers, procedural maps and river-crossing scarcity. The avoid list is the "Mini" prefix on a public title (so the repo name Mini-Waterways is fine for a school project but a poor public name), a look-alike shape set and palette, copying their UI layout, and any of their sound assets. Trademark status of "Mini Metro" and "Mini Motorways" was not looked up, so check a registry before publishing anything under a "Mini" name; a distinct title and an original visual language (for example topographic contour lines, brass and ink tones, lock gates as the signature icon) solves the problem and fits the project's name.

## Open questions and a phased build plan

Decisions the research cannot settle: whether the barge no-repath rule is wanted (the Motorways reviewer calls it unfair); whether lock count or tile count is the scarcer weekly currency; how elevation is made legible in 2D (contour shading, numbered levels, or lock icons only); whether the water budget is core or an optional mode; whether the project is a school deliverable, a portfolio piece or a public release, which changes the naming and licensing stakes; whether to keep the repo URL or create a new one; and what the target devices are, since touch support changes hit radii and the PWA scope. Empirically unknown until playtested: every number in the tuning paragraph above, plus how long a run lasts at a given spawn ramp. Worth a manual read the notes could not fetch: the DPC "Expanding the Miniverse" GDC talk, the full Gfinity making-of for Motorways, and the Giant Bomb Peter Curry interview.

| Phase | Goal | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Foundation | Fresh Vite and strict TypeScript project; Pages workflow on `main`; `typecheck`, `lint`, `test` in CI; config module for all tuning values | A blank canvas deploys from CI with a passing test |
| 1. Tracer bullet | Seeded sim; flat grid, one wharf and one quay colour, canal tiles, barges with A*, a quay ring timer and game over | A run starts, a barge delivers, a failed quay ends it, and a golden-replay test passes |
| 2. Escalation | Spawn scheduler with ramp and clustering/free-footprint rules; multiple colours; weekly clock and two-card choice; score | A run escalates to a natural loss in minutes without balance edits |
| 3. Water identity | Procedural elevation and rivers; locks with cycle time, queues, opposing-traffic bonus; side ponds, lifts, dredger as weekly options; locks shown legibly | Every seed forces locks, and each weekly option has a real use case |
| 4. Feel | Web Audio layer (scale, quantised events, tension pad, ring cue); palette module and colourblind option; touch input; pause and speed; autosave | A first-time player on a phone understands the goal without text |
| 5. Depth and release | Summit water budget; Endless and Expert modes; seeded daily; replay export; optional PWA; naming and trademark check | A shareable build with a distinct title and an IP check done |

Phase 1 should take priority over any polish, because the research shows prototypes in this genre commonly fail on the escalation curve and the weekly choice layer, not on visuals. Every phase ends with a playtest and a retuning pass, since the numbers are estimates.

## Conclusion

The central finding is that the genre's value lives in a short, copyable structure (autonomous agents, scarce crossings, weekly opportunity cost, local overload with a timer, audio feedback) while its tuning is undocumented, so the project's real risk is balance rather than implementation. That favours a build order that gets a seeded, replayable simulation running first and uses deterministic replays to iterate on numbers cheaply. It also makes the lock the right signature mechanic: it has a real-world cycle time, it creates the same junction-style bottleneck that makes Motorways tense, and it gives the "break the geography" resource a water-native form.

What the rebuild implies going forward: the old repo contributes almost nothing but a deploy pipeline and a name, so the decision to rebuild costs little. The one task that should not be left to phase 5 is the naming and trademark question, because it determines the repo name, the visual direction and whether the project can ever be shown publicly, and the evidence shows DPC polices close clones.
